# Dieter Rams scorecard

1. **Good design is innovative — Score: 2/3**
   Evidence: Familiar portfolio patterns are refreshed with restrained, data-backed personal feeds ([evidence](01-evidence.md#1-innovative)).
   Justification: It improves an established format without introducing a genuinely new interaction pattern.

2. **Good design makes a product useful — Score: 2/3**
   Evidence: Work and contact are direct, but the primary independent product is not clickable and service navigation disappears on mobile ([evidence](01-evidence.md#2-useful)).
   Justification: The main task is supported, though two avoidable obstacles weaken evaluation and conversion.

3. **Good design is aesthetic — Score: 2/3**
   Evidence: The grid, neutrals, responsive behavior, and accent are coherent, while type/spacing/color values are broader than a tight token system ([evidence](01-evidence.md#3-aesthetic)).
   Justification: The system is visibly cohesive in code, with minor consolidation opportunities that require a visual regression check.

4. **Good design makes a product understandable — Score: 1/3**
   Evidence: The overall hierarchy is legible, but multiple client-facing terms are jargon and structural heading/landmark issues remain ([evidence](01-evidence.md#4-understandable)).
   Justification: Several labels require domain knowledge, so a nontechnical client cannot decode every important claim immediately.

5. **Good design is unobtrusive — Score: 3/3**
   Evidence: No modal, badge, idle animation, or decorative chrome competes with the content ([evidence](01-evidence.md#5-unobtrusive)).
   Justification: The interface recedes and gives the work and writing visual priority.

6. **Good design is honest — Score: 1/3**
   Evidence: There are no dark patterns, but multiple absolute/freshness claims are not precisely supported by the site’s behavior or evidence ([evidence](01-evidence.md#6-honest)).
   Justification: More than one inflation or mismatch triggers the lower rubric score even though none appears intentionally deceptive.

7. **Good design is long-lasting — Score: 3/3**
   Evidence: Neutral editorial styling and conventional interactions avoid dated visual markers ([evidence](01-evidence.md#7-long-lasting)).
   Justification: The visual language should remain current without relying on a short-lived trend.

8. **Good design is thorough down to the last detail — Score: 2/3**
   Evidence: Core resilient and accessibility states exist, but focus consistency, contrast, landmarks, and feed-status clarity need refinement ([evidence](01-evidence.md#8-thorough)).
   Justification: The static-fallback model makes loading/error/disabled controls largely inapplicable; one cluster of accessibility finishing work remains rough.

9. **Good design is environmentally friendly — Score: 2/3**
   Evidence: JS and motion are lean, but five review thumbnails transfer 29.7 MB in the measured visit ([evidence](01-evidence.md#9-environmentally-friendly)).
   Justification: Runtime code is efficient and motion is gated, while image delivery creates a severe resource outlier.

10. **Good design is as little design as possible — Score: 2/3**
    Evidence: The surface is structurally lean, with a small number of repeated destinations and a dense review/tool section ([evidence](01-evidence.md#10-as-little-design-as-possible)).
    Justification: Nearly every element earns its place; at most two content/affordance reductions would improve focus.

**Total: 20/30**
